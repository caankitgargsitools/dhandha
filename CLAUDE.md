# Dhandha — handover notes for Claude Code

Dhandha (धंधा) is a multi-tenant SaaS "one stop business generation suite" being built by CA Ankit Garg to sell to other businesses.

It covers:
- tenders: crawl, read, fill and file
- a document Vault, so users are never asked for the same document twice
- annexures printed on the customer's letterhead with their signature
- lead scraping
- a CRM
- social posts and AI video
- central ML shared across users
- billing: a small subscription plus pay-as-you-go credits

## Ground rules from the owner
1. **Cost order is always free → cheaper → costlier — for every AI or paid tool, in every project.** Where several tiers exist, layer them: a free tool or reader first (rules engine, Gemini free tier, …); only if it cannot do the job, the cheapest paid one; then costlier ones. Never start at a paid tier when a free one can try.
   - Tender reading: the free rule engine first (`lib/tenderRules.js`), then Gemini Flash free tier, then Gemini Flash-Lite free tier (Flash is often busy), then Claude Haiku 5.5, then Haiku 4.5, then Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`lib/ai.js` `aiTiers`; each failure, refusal or cut-off answer falls through to the next; spend per call in `ai_usage`). `runJson` keeps the chain inside a time budget (default 240 s; pages with AI steps have `maxDuration = 300`): the free tier waits at most 30 s when paid tiers follow, and a tier starts only with ≥15 s left. `aiDocument` (`lib/tenderPdf.js`) picks what the AI gets: the whole PDF (≤18 MB, ≤100 pages), else real extracted text, else the first pages of a big scanned PDF.
   - The Gemini free tier may train on what it receives, so it only ever gets public tender documents (`private:false`). Customer private data goes to paid tiers only (`private:true`).
2. **Own crawler only.** No aggregator or third-party tender data services. Code is in `crawler/` (Node + Playwright; GePNIC, GeM and Gujarat nProcure adapters — `crawler/src/adapters/nprocure.js` reads the public home-page table's own data call `beforeLoginTenderTableList`, ≈3,400 tenders, 10 a page, `NPROCURE_PAGES`; India-only). Never bypass or auto-solve captchas: where a page needs one, the crawler shows the picture to a platform admin (Admin → "Captcha needed" card, `crawler/src/captcha.js`, `crawler_captchas`) and a person types it.
3. **Signatures need the admin's TOTP code.** Signature and seal go on a PDF only after the admin enters it, and every use is logged (`log_signature_use`). Drafts carry a "DRAFT – NOT SIGNED" watermark.
4. **No credentials in chat.** Never ask for passwords or keys.
5. **Prefer non-destructive database changes.** Disable roles, revoke flags or ban users instead of deleting rows.
6. **Dummy data must be removable.** Every demo row is registered in `public.demo_seed(table_name,row_id)` so it can be removed before go-live.
8. **Pending list, every day.** `PENDING.md` lists what waits on the owner and what Claude is doing. Do everything possible yourself; list only what truly needs the owner. A scheduled task checks it every morning (09:23 IST): verify what is done (database, GitHub, live checks), finish what you can, update the file, and send the list to the owner in chat, asking them to say what they have done. Follow this in all development.
9. **Keep working; don't wait (28 Oct).** Keep developing all the time. Keep studying competitors (CRM, lead finders, tenders, AI calling) and put every feature they have that we don't in `COMPETITOR_IDEAS.md` (pending the owner's approval; ask about them one at a time). The owner has given standing approval for normal work (access, tools, database changes). If a design/feature decision is needed and no answer comes within about a minute, move on to the next item and ask again when the owner is back. When input is needed, send a phone push (PushNotification) starting "Papa, I am waiting for your input: …".
7. **Answer in chat.** The owner wants answers inline in chat rather than as separate documents. Ask questions one at a time, with Yes/No/Skip or multiple-choice options — use the AskUserQuestion tool (buttons), keep messages very short.
